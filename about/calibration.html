CALIBRATION · THE LEVEL-100 FORMULA

The level-100 formula — measure the level, never feel it

A proof that fails is a finding; a feeling that fails is a mood. This page replaces "I’m roughly at level 100 out of 100" with a weighted score over five channels of linkable evidence, ten gates with objective pass conditions, and a tool that computes your level and prints an audit log you can hand to someone else.

~28 min working tool: self-assessment 10 gates · 5 channels rubric version 2026-10-01
no self-attestation evidence pointer Teach gate confidence interval reproduced by a stranger 18-month decay monotone gate floor quarterly audit

1 · Why level must be measured #

You spent seven years in an audit function. You already know the whole argument, so it takes one paragraph: a claim without evidence is not a finding. An auditor does not record "the enrollment file looked compliant". They record the file, the rule, the test that was run, the result, and the date. Anyone later can re-run the test. If they cannot, the finding is withdrawn.

Skill self-assessment fails in exactly the way an unaudited file fails — in four specific ways, all of which will happen to you on the first pass of this page:

The recency illusion

You finish L26 and feel like you understand scaling laws. Feeling is a function of the last hour, not of retained capability. A claim made in the last hour decays; a repo made two years ago does not pretend to be recent. That is why the rubric has an 18-month decay rule and the feeling does not.

The fluency trap

Reading code you can read is not knowing what the code computes. Recognising loss.backward() produces a strong sense of competence and precisely zero derivational ability. The rubric answers this by making the Teach channel a recording, and by making the Concept channel a held-out check rather than a read-and-nod.

Ready access to transcripts, not evidence

You can always write "yes I have done it". That is self-attestation — the accused writing the verdict. The rubric answers this with a hard rule: no point without an evidence pointer — a link, a repo, a commit SHA, or a measured number produced by a procedure someone else could repeat.

The national-average illusion

Everyone in this field must first learn the material. Being a relative generalist and being a specialist feel different in your own head only when you measure them separately. The rubric separates them because you are deliberately uneven — high Build, high Reproduce, thin Measure and Teach. An average would erase the three facts that matter most. The weighted sum plus the gate floor keeps them visible.

The audit lens, applied to yourself: your portfolio currently contains several strong claims — 76.47% vs 25.49%, zero false positives, 22 of 25 exact — each reported as a point. An auditor would reject those as findings and hold them as observations pending a measurement procedure, a sample size, and an interval. On this rubric they earn real Build and Reproduce credit and only partial Measure credit. That is not a criticism; it is what the rubric is for.

2 · The five channels and the formula #

Every level in this course rests on five independent kinds of evidence. They are separate channels rather than one blended score because they fail independently: a person can build without measuring, or measure without teaching. The weights below are a deliberate design choice, not a neutral default, and section 2.4 explains each one.

ChannelWeightWhat counts as evidenceWhat does not
1 · Concept 20% Self-check items passed on held-out questions; hand-derived results that match a reference implementation to a stated tolerance; a written derivation with the step where you got stuck marked. Having read the lesson. Having watched the video. "Understood the gist".
2 · Build 30% A working artefact in a repo at a specific commit: a script that prints numbers, a test suite, a harness, a service that ran. The commit SHA is the pointer. A plan, a sketch, a prompt, a repo whose README claims a result you cannot see printed anywhere.
3 · Teach 20% A recorded 5-minute explanation to someone who does not know the field, plus their paraphrase of it and what they could not repeat. Internal understanding. A blog post about the topic in general. A slide without an audience.
4 · Measure 20% A number produced by a declared procedure, reported with a sample size and a confidence interval, plus the falsifier: what result would have disconfirmed the design. "It works." "Much better." "Zero failures" with no denominator. Any single run.
5 · Reproduce 10% A log, diff, or screen recording of a stranger (or a clean container) running your artefact from your written instructions and getting your number. "You can just run it." A README that was written by the same person who never re-ran it.

2.1 · The scoring function

Each channel has a set of checks. A check is worth its points only when its evidence pointer validates. With pi the points actually validated in channel i and Pi the maximum available in that channel:

LevelScore = ∑i=1..5 wi · (pi / Pi) w = (0.20, 0.30, 0.20, 0.20, 0.10) for Concept, Build, Teach, Measure, Reproduce. Max score = 100 when every check validates.

The raw weighted score is not the level. The level is the highest gate your evidence clears, and gates impose a floor:

Level = max { n ∈ 1..10 : LevelScore ≥ Sn  ∧  every mandatory channel of gate n is cleared } Sn = (15, 22, 30, 38, 46, 54, 63, 73, 84, 95) — thresholds in section 4. The gate floor means a score cannot buy a level past a missing channel: with 90 points but zero Measure evidence you are still capped at L4.

Two numbers, one verdict. The score says how much evidence you have accumulated. The gate floor says which evidence you do not have. The verdict is the smaller of the two. That is the whole design — it is the same two-part logic as "the transaction matched every field and the required documentation was present".

2.2 · Worked arithmetic (do this by hand before touching the tool)

Every check is worth 4 points and each channel holds 5 checks, so 1 point of a channel = 5 percentage points of that channel's ratio. 2.3 · Where you probably sit today — a hypothesis, not a fact

The honest hypothesis is L6–L7, and the range exists because the only genuinely uncertain inputs are Measure and Teach. Build is exceptional by any standard (an audited bug-bounty gate, a hash-chained ledger outside the writable workspace, a validated simulator, a controlled six-way benchmark). Reproduce is strong in the "I can show you the ledger" sense and weak in the "a stranger re-ran it" sense. Measure is thin: numbers exist, intervals and sample sizes mostly do not. Teach is thinnest: there is engineering writing, but not much five-minute explanation to a non-specialist with a paraphrase logged.

Do not accept this estimate. Run the tool in section 5, then ask one other person to check your pointers. The number you should trust is the one produced after someone else looks at your evidence list — because that is the only number an employer will ever compute about you too.

2.4 · Why these five weights

  • Build at 30% — the largest single weight, because an artefact in a repo is the cheapest evidence for a stranger to verify and the most resistant to inflation. Code either runs or it does not. If a whole course were Build only, the learner would optimise for volume: twenty half-finished demos.
  • Concept at 20% — necessary but cheap to fake to yourself. A wrong mental model and a right one produce identical feelings until a held-out question or a hand-derivation separates them. Hence a full fifth, paid only against held-out checks.
  • Teach at 20% — the Feynman gate. It is the only channel that fails on a still-present misunderstanding while the author is confident. Explaining in five recorded minutes to a non-specialist is the cheapest known detector of gaps that reading cannot see. It also produces the artefact that is most scarce in this portfolio.
  • Measure at 20% — the same weight as Teach, and the second thinnest channel. A system that works on your machine, today, at 2 p.m., is not a system; a system with an interval and a falsifier is. This is the channel that turns your portfolio's strong claims into findings.
  • Reproduce at 10% — deliberately lower, not because it matters less, but because it is expensive (it needs a second person) and because it is largely a property of your Build discipline. A person with strong Build who writes setup instructions down and a person who does not will diverge naturally here. Keep it scarce so that earning it means something.

3 · Five rules that make it non-gameable #

A rubric is only as good as its resistance to the person using it. These five rules are load-bearing — if you weaken one, delete the page.

(a) No self-attestation — every point needs a pointer

Each check contains a mandatory evidence pointer: a URL, a repository path with a commit SHA, a file path and line range, or a measured number together with the procedure that produced it. A check whose pointer is empty scores zero even if it is ticked. The pointer must be resolvable by someone other than you: "I built this" is not a pointer; github.com/you/repo@a1b2c3d is.

A pointer is valid if a stranger can do this with it: 1. open it — the link resolves, or the path exists at that SHA 2. see the claim — the number/log exists there, not in a README paraphrase 3. repeat it — the procedure is written down or the command is in the repo Anything shorter than 1-2-3 is a note, not evidence. Record it as a note and take 0 points.

(b) The Teach gate is a recorded five-minute explanation

Not a blog post about the topic. Not a slide deck. A recording of at least five minutes in which you explain one mechanism to a person who does not work in the field, followed by their one-paragraph paraphrase in their own words and a list of the things they could not repeat. A teaching claim requires both artefacts. If the listener's paraphrase is vague, the gate is failed — the failure is the finding.

This is the cheapest known detector of the fluency trap (section 1). You will watch yourself say "it's just like a lookup" about attention and then be unable to answer "so what is the model doing when there is nothing to look up?" That stumble is worth more than another lesson finished.

(c) The Measure gate requires a number with an interval

Every measurement claim requires four fields: the procedure (command or script and the data it ran on), the sample size, the point estimate with a 95% confidence interval, and the falsifier — the pre-declared result that would have disconfirmed the design. A number without an interval is a raw observation and earns nothing. This is consistent with L88, which builds the harness.

CI95% (≈ Wilson or percentile-bootstrap)  →  report as  estimate ± half-width  or  [lower, upper], with n. Example of the difference: "76.47% vs 25.49%" is an observation. "76.47% [63.2, 86.0] vs 25.49% [16.5, 37.0], n = 51 per arm" is a finding, because the intervals do not overlap and the n is stated.

(d) The Reproduce gate requires a stranger

Reproduction is a log or a screen recording of another person (or a clean environment you never tuned while running) executing your written setup and obtaining your number within a stated tolerance. The required artefact is the log plus the exact commit SHA that was used. If someone had to ask you a question during the run, record the question — it is a finding about your documentation, not a favour.

(e) Decay — evidence older than 18 months counts at half weight

Each evidence pointer carries a dated field. Points from evidence older than 18 months are multiplied by 0.5, and nothing older than 36 months counts at all (it stays in the log for context, at zero points). The comparison date is always the date the assessment was run, never the date of an earlier run.

In the section 5 tool the unit of decay is the pointer, recorded per channel: you tick the check, then enter in the decayed box how many of that channel's validated points are still inside 18 months. pi is then validated points plus half of the decayed ones, and the live formula display re-derives the channel ratio from those two numbers.

pi = (pointsi − di) + 0.5 · di   where di = decayed (18–36 month) points still valid in channel i Points older than 36 months are archived at zero. The tool in section 5 takes di as an input per channel and computes the ratio (pi / Pi) live — so a learning loop that never produces new artefacts visibly decays. The live formula display re-derives each channel's ratio from your inputs.
  • Building with no measurement. Symptom: the Build channel is full and Measure is empty; you have shipped a lot and cannot say how much better anything is. Caught by: gate floors at L6.
  • Reading with no teaching. Symptom: Concept is full, Teach is empty; the material feels understood and disappears in a conversation. Caught by: the L5 Teach floor.
  • Claim inflation with no pointers. Symptom: a high score whose evidence box contains sentences rather than links. Caught by: every ticked check with an empty pointer counts as zero, visible in the log.
  • Self-reproducing. Symptom: Reproduce points earned by running your own script twice. Caught by: the stranger requirement in rule (d).
  • Frozen portfolio. Symptom: score stops moving after month three. Caught by: 18-month decay — frozen evidence is disposable work.

  2. Independent reproduction by a stranger, twice, without contact. Two parties you have never corresponded with reproduce the result from the published specification alone. Neither asks you a question. Their writeups state the obtained number and it falls inside your declared interval.

At L9 people reproduce your work when you are in the room. At L10 people you have never met reproduce it from the document, cite it correctly, and the citation is still accurate a year later. This is the gate that cannot be passed alone, cannot be passed by effort in a single quarter, and is the only one where failing for a year while a reproduction is pending is the expected cost.

What L10 is not. L10 is not "published a paper". A preprint nobody cites earns the citation condition zero. L10 is not "went viral". A blog post with 100k views and no reproduction earns condition 2 zero. L10 is not "a large project". Size is not the variable; external, contact-free validation is.

Why L10 is defined this way: because it is the exact thing you spent seven years being able to spot in everyone else’s filing. A claim that only its author can check is not a finding. L10 is the first gate where your own work passes that test, and it is the only gate whose evidence you cannot generate alone.

5 · The self-assessment tool #

Tick every check whose evidence pointer is filled in and resolvable. A ticked check with an empty pointer is scored as zero and flagged in the log. The tool stores your state in localStorage, computes channels, score, gate floor and level, and emits a copyable evidence-log JSON you can paste into a quarterly review or hand to a reviewer.

How the floor works in the tool. The score selects the top candidate gate from the thresholds. Then each candidate gate is checked against its mandatory channels. The level is the highest gate whose threshold and mandatory channels both hold: L5 requires Teach > 0, L6 and above require Measure > 0, L7 and above require Teach ≥ 7 (i.e. a second recording), L8 and above require Reproduce ≥ 5 (an external re-run), L9 and above require Reproduce ≥ 9 and a dated pre-registration, and L10 requires all five channels full plus the external-citation and uncontacted-reproduction checklist in section 4.

5.1 · Pointer validity check

For each ticked check, paste its pointer into this box. If a stranger cannot do the three steps in section 3(a) with it, untick the check. The empty-pointer count is the number of points you are about to claim dishonestly.

Guidelines: a repo path must include a commit SHA — github.com/u/r@a1b2c3d; a measurement must include the command and the n — python eval.py --set held_out.txt → n=240; a lesson artifact must name the lesson — track-b/26 + fit.py, held-out rel-err 4.1%.

6 · The printable audit and the quarterly review #

Print this page (browser print, or the button in the tool) and keep the checklist physically. A rubric you only ever view on the screen you are also working on is a rubric that gets edited by mood.

The rule that matters most in the review: a score that does not fall after a rigorous review means the review was not rigorous. The honest pattern over four quarters is small, lumpy gains with occasional drops when old evidence decays — that shape is the rubric working.

6.2 · Printable checklist

Pointers & integrity

  • Every ticked check has a pointer (URL, repo@SHA, path:lines, or command + n).
  • Every pointer opens without private access.
  • Every number exists at the pointer, not in a paraphrase.
  • No shared or borrowed artefacts counted twice.
  • Dates are recorded for every pointer.
  • Older than 18 months: halved. Older than 36 months: archived.
  • Previous quarter’s JSON is preserved and diffed.

Teach gate

  • Recording is at least 5 minutes long.
  • The listener does not work in the field.
  • A one-paragraph paraphrase in the listener’s words is stored.
  • The list of things the listener could not repeat is stored.
  • The idea was stated without notes or slides.
  • For L9–10: a third recording shows the listener teaching it correctly.

Measure gate

  • The procedure (command or script) is recorded.
  • Sample size n is stated.
  • Point estimate plus 95% interval is reported.
  • The falsifier was declared before the run and dated.
  • Negative and non-significant results are reported, not deleted.
  • For L9–10: the evaluation is pre-registered.

Reproduce gate

  • A clean environment, or a person who is not the author, ran it.
  • Their log is stored with the commit SHA used.
  • The README they used was not written after their run.
  • Every question they asked is recorded as a finding.
  • Their obtained number is inside the declared interval.
  • For L10: two strangers, no contact, both logs attached.

6.3 · Applying it to the profile in this course

The honest hypothesis for the learner this course was written for is L6–L7, for exactly the reason visible in the profile: an unusually complete Build record, a self-reviewed rather than externally re-run Reproduce record, point estimates without intervals in Measure, and almost no recorded Teach. Run the tool, and treat the result as a benchmark to beat rather than a grade — L100 schedules the whole thing.

In one line: your level is the highest gate whose evidence survives a stranger. Not the highest lesson you finished, not the hardest paper you read, not the strongest claim in your portfolio — the one a stranger can re-run.

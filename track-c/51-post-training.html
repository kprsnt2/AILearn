TRACK C · POST-TRAINING & REASONING

L51 · Post-training map: SFT → preferences → RL → RLHF vs RLVR

A model that is right 88% of the time fails in four structurally different ways, and each one needs a different signal, not a bigger fine-tune. This page turns "what do I tune?" into a six-rule function you can run on your own defect list.

~28 min difficulty ●●○○○ build: decision tree for what to tune
SFT preference pairs learned reward model verifiable reward KL tether reference policy rollout sampling audit bracket

1 · Why it exists #

A team ships a model that is right 88% of the time and still cannot be put behind an unattended agent, because the other 12% fails in four structurally different ways: it emits malformed JSON, it refuses a benign clinical question, it answers in a tone the compliance team rejects, and it states a fee schedule that does not exist. Four defects, one model, and the reflex is "fine-tune it". That reflex is what wastes a quarter.

Each of those four defects is a different supervision signal you do not have yet. Malformed output needs demonstrations of the format. Tone needs comparisons, not answers. Arithmetic needs a grader that can score an answer without a human. A missing fact needs retrieval, because no amount of tuning will put a client PDF into the weights. Post-training is not one process; it is a ladder where each rung requires you to have manufactured a signal the rung below cannot supply.

Post-training is signal engineering. You never "make the model better" — you install a measurable preference over outputs, and the method you pick is decided entirely by what kind of comparison you can manufacture cheaply and honestly: a demonstration, a pairwise label, a learned scorer, or a program that just runs the answer.

state as of 2026-10 — the current frontier mostly ships this ladder in the order SFT → RLVR → distillation, with preference learning used where the reward is taste rather than truth. DeepSeek-R1 (arXiv:2501.12948) is the clearest published instance: SFT cold-start, then GRPO against verifiable rewards, then rejection-sampled distillation into smaller models.

2 · The mental model #

The mental model: think of a raw pretrained model as a function that only knows Plausible Next Token. Post-training installs a second, cheap-to-evaluate function called a proxy for Good, and then pushes the policy uphill on the proxy while tethering it to the original distribution. Three things have to be in place: the proxy, the push, and the tether.

RungProxy for Good you must manufactureShelf lifeWhat it cannot fix
SFTA demonstration of a full correct response (human or stronger model)Per tokenAnything the demonstrator never demonstrates — including recovering from its own failure
Preference learningA pairwise label: A is better than B for this promptPer pairMagnitude. Order without scale; cannot distinguish "slightly worse" from "dangerous" unless you encode it
Reward model + PPOA scalar function trained on your pairs, then queried millions of timesPer query, freeAnything the pair dataset never constrained. A free query is also a free attack surface
Verifiable rewards + GRPOA program that returns 0/1 (unit tests, exact match, a type checker, a simulator)Per rolloutAnything you cannot write a checker for: style, safety, open-ended quality
DistillationAnother model's accepted outputsPer token, after filteringCeiling = teacher quality; you inherit its errors verbatim

What the ladder analogy gets right

You cannot skip a rung and keep the one above it. RL on top of an untrained base finds degenerate high-reward strings; DPO without a decent SFT reference drifts into losing the format entirely. Order is not stylistic, it is structural: each rung's reference policy is the rung below.

Where it breaks

Real pipelines are not strictly ordered. PPO itself contains an SFT-style cross-entropy term as a floor; RLVR is often mixed with SFT batches in one optimiser step; and "no tuning at all" is frequently the correct rung, because a prompt fix costs minutes and a training run costs a week of GPU time plus an eval rebuild.

Two invariants hold across every rung and are worth memorising:

  • Every rung needs a reference. SFT against the base distribution, DPO against the SFT policy, PPO/GRPO against a KL anchor. The reference is what makes "better" measurable instead of mushy.
  • Every rung optimises a proxy, so every rung is hackable. The proxy is a sample of what you could measure; the policy will find the region where the proxy and the truth disagree. Lesson 75 is about that failure; it is the reason the audit bracket exists.

3 · The mechanism #

All five methods reduce to one objective with different estimators. The general form is

maxθ   Ex∼πθ [ r(x) ]  −  β · KL(πθ ‖ πref) r is the proxy reward (a learned scalar or a grader), πref is the frozen reference policy, β is your willingness to drift.

The unconstrained optimum of that objective is closed-form and worth internalising, because every page in this track is about approximating it:

π*(y|x)  ∝  πref(y|x) · exp( r(x,y) / β ) Reweight the reference by exponentiated reward. When β→∞ you get the reference back; when β→0 you get the argmax of the proxy.

Now place each method against it:

MethodEstimator of rReference usedNeeds rollouts?Models in memory
SFTNone — the demonstration is the answerImplicit: you only train on the demonstrated tokensNo1
DPO (arXiv:2305.18290)Implicit inside the policy: the policy itself is reparameterised as a rewardThe SFT policy (frozen copy or the initial one)No — offline pairs2 (policy + frozen reference)
PPO (arXiv:1707.06347)A separate trained reward modelKL penalty against a frozen policyYes, cheap sampling then repeated epochs4 (policy, reference, reward, value/critic)
GRPO (arXiv:2402.03300)A grader, or a reward modelKL penalty against a frozen policyYes — G samples per prompt2 (policy, reference) + optional reward model

The whole of DPO follows from inverting the closed form. If π* ∝ πref exp(r/β), then solving for r gives r(x,y) = β log(π*(y|x)/πref(y|x)) + const. Substitute that into a Bradley–Terry comparison and the const cancels, so you can train the policy directly on pairs without ever building a reward model. That derivation is the entirety of lesson 55.

4 · Your hands #

Three experiments. The first is a decision procedure you can run on your own defect list today. The second shows what the KL tether actually costs and where it fails to protect you. The third is the ladder in miniature.

Experiment 2 — what the KL tether costs

A four-way behavioural choice, a reward that is a good-enough proxy, and a truth bracket the optimiser never sees. The exact KL-regularised optimum is computed directly, so there is no sampling noise in this experiment.

Experiment 3 — price a rung before you build it

Fill in this run card for a defect you actually own. Nothing here is a guess except your own estimates, and that is the point: an estimate written before training is a prediction you can be shown wrong about.

run card (copy into your repo, one per attempt) defect : <one sentence, with a frequency> cheapest prompt : <what a 30-minute prompt fix buys on the eval> owner of signal : <demonstration | pairwise label | learned scorer | program> proxy : <the exact function you will maximise> audit bracket : <the eval you will NOT optimise, run blind> reference : <checkpoint path + sha256> KL budget : <max nats before you stop> predicted delta : <number you expect on the bracket> falsifier : <result that makes you abandon the run>

If audit bracket or falsifier is blank, you are not ready to start the run. A run you cannot falsify will confirm whatever you hoped for.

5 · Failure modes #

  • Tuning instead of prompting. Symptom: an SFT run improves the eval by a few points, and the same gain is available from a system-prompt rewrite that took one afternoon. The test is cheap: build the few-shot prompt first and only train if it hits a ceiling.
  • Skipping SFT to run preference learning on a messy base. Symptom: DPO loss decreases monotonically while format compliance falls, because the policy found a way to be "preferred" by degrading the reference behaviour it was never taught to keep.
  • Reward model treated as ground truth. Symptom: reward climbs smoothly every checkpoint and the independently-graded eval is flat or down. This is the signature of reward hacking (lesson 75), not of a training bug.
  • Rung chosen by fashion rather than by signal type. Symptom: weeks spent building a reward model for a task where a unit test already gives 0/1 for free, or conversely an RLVR attempt on a task with no checker at all.
  • No stale-reference discipline. Symptom: KL to the reference rises far beyond the plateau and the model starts emitting long, confident, wrong answers — the well-known length and verbosity drift. state as of 2026-10
  • No audit bracket. Symptom: you cannot answer "which capability changed and by how much" when someone asks, because the only measurement in the loop was the proxy you optimised.

Before any post-training run, write down four numbers and put them in the run card: (1) the proxy you will optimise, (2) the independent bracket you will never optimise, (3) the reference checkpoint by hash, (4) the maximum KL you will accept before stopping. If any of the four is missing, the run is unfalsifiable by construction.

7 · Self-check #

Q1. A defect is: the model returns JSON with a trailing comma 40% of the time. Which rung, and what is the smallest experiment that could falsify your answer?

Reveal answer

SFT (format/schema rule fires first), because the defect is a format miss and demonstrations of the exact schemaare the cheapest signal. Falsifier: a constrained-decoding or schema-validating wrapper fixes it with zero training —run that first. If grammar-constrained decoding removes the failure entirely, the correct rung is no tuning andpost-training would have been pure waste.

Q2. Your pair dataset says A > B for 10,000 pairs. Two policies give identical rankings on all 10,000 pairs but different probability mass. Can DPO distinguish them?

Reveal answer

Only through the reference term. The DPO logit is β[log(πθ(A)/πref(A)) − log(πθ(B)/πref(B))], which depends onthe policy's probabilities, not just the ranking — so two policies with the same declared ranking but different mass do getdifferent losses, weighted by how far they move from the reference. What DPO cannot see is anything the pairs never compared:a behaviour C that no pair mentions is unconstrained. That is the structural gap, not the mass.

Q3. Quantitative: at β = 0.5, what reward difference between two behaviours is exactly offset by a KL cost of 0.4 nats?

Reveal answer

Optimality means Δreward = β · ΔKL. With β = 0.5 and ΔKL = 0.4, Δreward = 0.2. Any behaviour whose reward edgeis smaller than 0.2 cannot justify 0.4 nats of drift. That is the arithmetic behind choosing β: it prices nats of drift in reward units.

Q4. Diagnostic: reward rises every checkpoint and the held-out graded eval is flat. Name the two hypotheses and the one measurement that separates them.

Reveal answer

(a) Reward hacking: the policy found proxy-true/truth-false outputs. (b) Eval contamination or a broken bracket: the graded evalitself is leaking or measuring the wrong thing. The separating measurement: take the checkpoint's outputs, strip any tells of style,and have a blind human or an independent model grade a fixed sample; in parallel, inspect the highest-reward outputs for proxies(length, hedging phrases, citation-like strings). If the highest-reward outputs are visibly degenerate, it is (a); if the sample grades wellbut the harness reports flat, it is (b).

Q5. Why does every rung in the ladder need a reference policy or checkpoint, and what specifically goes wrong if you delete it?

Reveal answer

Without a reference, "better" has no definition and no tether: the policy is free to satisfy the proxy by rewriting the wholebehavioural contract. Concretely, delete the KL term from PPO or the reference term from DPO and the policy drifts to degeneratehigh-proxy outputs — runaway length, sycophantic agreement, or format collapse — because those are the cheapest directions in which theproxy rises. The reference is what converts an unbounded maximisation into a trust region you can audit.
